Keyframe Selection for Long-Video MLLMs

Same start for all: a scorer gives one number per frame. The unique idea is how each turns scores → a picked subset. Tap a method. · slm-lab, 2026-07

The map

Every method: scorer (SigLIP / BLIP / CLIP) → one relevance number per frame. The scorer is commodity — SigLIP vs BLIP is a ±2-pt detail. All the intellectual content is in the selection algorithm. Our baseline = plain top-k (take the highest numbers). It has two weaknesses everyone else attacks:

  • W1 — redundancy: top-k grabs near-duplicate frames all on one moment.
  • W2 — blind & expensive: scores every frame, trusts the raw number.

baseline

Ours — plain top-k

Unique trick: none — take the k highest-scoring frames. The honest baseline every other method is defined against.
How it works
  1. SigLIP so400m scores every 1-fps (or 2s-clip) frame vs the question.
  2. Sort descending, take the top k (e.g. 8, 32).
  3. Feed to the answerer (GPT).
frames sorted by score; top-k = the brightest, wherever they fall (can bunch)
Weakness
  • W1: near-duplicate frames on one moment eat the budget.
  • W2: scores every frame; a frame that merely looks like the query beats the one that answers it.
encoder: SigLIP so400mtrain-free: yes result: LVB topk-8 0.74 vs uniform 0.52 (n=50, 600s)
A · Spread Feb 25 · the baseline

AKS — recursive time-binning

Unique trick: split the timeline into bins and force picks across them → guarantees temporal coverage, can't dump all frames on one moment.
How it works
  1. BLIP/CLIP scores each frame vs the prompt.
  2. Split video into bins. In each bin, compare its top score to a threshold.
  3. Strong bin → take its best frame. Weak/ambiguous → recursively split & spread.
  4. Balances "high score" against "cover the whole timeline."
encoder: BLIP/CLIPtrain-free: yes result: LVB 62.7 vs 58.9 uniformrole: shared baseline
A · Spread Oct 25

AdaRD-Key — log-determinant diversity

Unique trick: the determinant of picked-frame embeddings = geometric "volume." Big only if picks point different directions; a near-duplicate adds ~0 → gets skipped.
How it works
  1. R(f) = BLIP-2 ITM probability (relevance).
  2. Add frames greedily, each maximizing R(i) + λ·log det(Gram of picks).
  3. log-det = diversity in feature space; λ tuned adaptively.
  4. Gate: if best relevance < 0.4 → diversity-only (scores too noisy to trust).
encoder: BLIP-2 ITMtrain-free: yes result: LVB 62.9 vs AKS 62.7note: no encoder ablation
A · Spread Mar 26 · uses SigLIP

Adaptive Greedy — two encoders, facility-location

Unique trick: split the job across two encoders — SigLIP judges relevance, DINOv2 (pure vision) judges "is the whole video visually covered." Greedy with a (1−1/e) guarantee.
How it works
  1. F(S) = α·Relevance(S) + β·Coverage(S).
  2. Relevance = SigLIP query cosine (like us).
  3. Coverage = DINOv2 facility-location: every unpicked frame should be near some picked frame.
  4. Greedy add, submodular → (1−1/e) optimal. 1-fps pool ≤1000 frames.
encoder: SigLIP + DINOv2train-free: yes result: MLVU K=10 64.48 vs AKS 62.31validates our SigLIP

LDDR — DPP diversity + dynamic resolution

Unique trick: after picking diverse frames (DPP), give important frames high resolution and others low-res / pruned → spend the token budget on pixels, not just frame count.
How it works
  1. Linear-DPP selects important + diverse frames (determinant = anti-redundancy).
  2. Score each picked frame's importance (query-aware).
  3. Allocate resolution: hi-res for important, lo-res/prune for the rest.
  4. Same token budget → more of it on the frames that matter.
encoder: CLIPtrain-free: yes result: +2.5 budget-constrained · 3× faster than DPPnew lever: resolution
B · Cheap Nov 25

Focus — bandit, coarse-to-fine

Unique trick: treat scoring itself as a budget. Clips = slot-machine arms; glance at all cheaply, then spend the rest zooming into promising clips — with a math guard (UCB) against dropping a good clip on bad luck.
How it works
  1. Split video into 16s clips = bandit arms.
  2. Coarse: score a few frames in every clip (cheap glance everywhere).
  3. Fine: spend remaining scoring on top-α clips by Bernstein UCB — undersampled clip gets a wide uncertainty band, stays optimistically in the running.
  4. Keep top clips by average score; sample frames from them.
Weak spot (our edge)

Clip scored by its mean → a one-frame answer inside a 16s clip is averaged down by ~15 irrelevant frames. UCB guards unlucky sampling, not a genuinely diluted mean. Our top-k on 2s clips doesn't dilute.

encoder: BLIP ITMtrain-free: yes result: LVB 63.5 · +11.9 on >20min · <2% frames
C · Right thing Apr 26 · trained

Query-Conditioned Evidential — similarity → evidence

Unique trick: cosine picks frames that look like the query. They instead keep frames that change the MLLM's answer — formalized as mutual information I(S;O∣Q). Learned, not matched.
How it works
  1. Goal: max I(S ; O | Q) — picked frames S should be informative about output O given query Q.
  2. Can't search combinatorially → submodular upper bound → independent frame scoring.
  3. Train a ~10M evidence scorer (frozen CLIP + causal-window temporal aggregator + query gate + InfoNCE) on the new Seek-173K dataset (annotated evidence segments).

= our blind-baseline critique, formalized before us. Breaks frozen-encoder purity (it trains). Fails on audio + timestamp-grounded queries.

C · Right thing May 26 · query-free

Swift Sampling — temporal surprise

Unique trick: score a frame by how much it deviates from its neighbors (Taylor-series temporal derivative). Sudden change = "surprise" = keep. No query needed.
How it works
  1. Compute higher-order temporal derivatives via finite differences (Taylor series).
  2. High derivative = visual novelty vs surrounding frames → high score.
  3. Cheap — no optical flow, no dense features, no query, no training.

= our "distinctness" axis, formalized — but query-free, so it finds change, not your question's answer. That's its ceiling and our contrast.

encoder: CLIPtrain-free: yes result: up to +12.5 on long, limited budget
D · Compress Jun 26 · closest to our thesis

AdaCodec — predictive visual codec

Unique trick: don't pick k — run a codec: per frame decide keep-full / compress / drop. Every frame contributes, at a token cost proportional to its information.
How it works
  1. Score each frame's information/predictability.
  2. Redundant frames → squeeze to a few tokens (don't discard).
  3. Informative frames → keep at full token cost.
  4. Training-free, sits in front of the MLLM like a video codec.

= "compression buys cost" as a product — our exact pitch, already built. Headline: 1/7 token budget (32k vs 224k) beats the baseline; TTFT 9.26s → 1.62s.

encoder: —train-free: yes result: 1/7 tokens · 82% faster · 11 benches
