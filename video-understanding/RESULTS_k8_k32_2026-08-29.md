# LVB-600s selector matrix, k=8 vs k=32 — single environment

Run 2026-08-29, AWS g6e.xlarge (L40S), instance i-0a5066e906b575cf0.
Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, subs OFF, batch_size=1, LongCLIP scorer.
All 12 arms in ONE environment; n=412/412 unique qids each; common set = 412.

Motivation: the review panel's circularity charge (R13) — the paper's ordering is
established at a single budget (k=8). This tests whether it survives at k=32.

## Paired McNemar (exact, two-sided), significant results only

k=8, vs uniform — ALL selectors win:
  topk  -6.07pt p=.0165 | aks -6.31pt p=.0120 | focus -5.34pt p=.0246
  dppmm -8.50pt p=.00049 | omp -9.95pt p=9.2e-05

k=8, selector vs selector: NOTHING significant.
  best contrast omp vs topk +3.88pt p=.097 (ns); omp vs dppmm +1.46pt p=.50 (ns)

k=32, vs uniform — only three win:
  aks -5.10pt p=.0125 | dppmm -7.52pt p=.00019 | omp -4.85pt p=.0265
  topk -3.40pt p=.175 (ns)  | focus -0.24pt p=1.0 (ns)

k=32, selector vs selector: only vs FOCUS.
  aks vs focus +4.85pt p=.0265 | dppmm vs focus +7.28pt p=.00054 | omp vs focus +4.61pt p=.0295
  dppmm vs omp +2.67pt p=.161 (ns) | dppmm vs topk +4.13pt p=.053 (ns) | aks vs omp +0.24pt p=1.0 (ns)

## Findings

1. NO selector-vs-selector difference is significant at k=8. The paper's k=8
   ordering (omp > dppmm > aks > topk > focus) is entirely within noise. What IS
   significant at k=8 is that every selector beats uniform.

2. The rank order is NOT budget-invariant: OMP goes 1st -> 3rd, dppmm 2nd -> 1st.
   But since no pairwise selector gap is significant at either budget, the
   reordering is itself within noise. Honest statement: the ordering is
   unidentified at both budgets, not "reversed".

3. Selection-vs-uniform WEAKENS at k=32: top-k (p=.175) and FOCUS (p=1.0) no
   longer beat uniform, though aks/dppmm/omp still do. Uniform gains +6.80pt from
   k=8->k=32 while omp gains only +1.70pt — the selection advantage shrinks as
   budget grows, consistent with the saturation already documented.

4. AKS DEGENERATES TO TOP-K AT k=8. Its picks fall within 2s of the top-k picks
   for 99.8% of frames (exact-match 27.4%); at k=32 only 32.2% are within 2s.
   Consequence: k8_aks vs k8_topk McNemar is b=0, c=1 — 411/412 identical answers.
   At k=8, AKS and top-k are not distinct methods in effect. Any k=8-only table
   that lists them as separate rows is reporting one method twice.
   Tolerance overlap (fraction of picks matched within t):
     pair            exact   <=2s    <=5s   <=10s
     topk|aks  k8    0.274   0.998   1.000   1.000
     topk|aks  k32   0.084   0.322   0.347   0.407
     topk|focus k8   0.037   0.270   0.397   0.507
     topk|dppmm k8   0.086   0.329   0.376   0.433

## Caveats

- Environment drift: these k=8 numbers run ~0.7pt BELOW the previously banked
  k=8 values (topk .6068 vs banked .6141; uniform .5461 vs .5534), and the gate
  found OMP +1.45pt ABOVE its banked value. Cross-referencing these to the old
  banked table is invalid; all comparisons here are within this one environment.
- 600s bin only. 3600s not run.
- k8_omp is the gate run (gate_omp_lc_600_k8), same picks/task/settings.
